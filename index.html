An online cognitive study · about 5 minutes

When do people trust the AI, and when do they push back?

TrustLab is a small interactive study on human–AI decision making. You will complete a short cognitive task where an AI assistant offers answers with varying confidence — and then see how your pattern compares to the aggregate. All responses stay on your device unless you choose to share.

Run the study Read the methods

no accounts · no cookies · 10 trials · runs entirely in your browser

01

You run a task

Answer 10 short trivia items. Each time, an AI assistant offers an answer with a stated confidence level. You decide whether to accept it or override with your own answer.

02

We measure calibration

Behind the scenes, we log your decision, response time, and accuracy. The design lets us estimate how much AI confidence sways your judgement — independent of whether the AI is actually right.

03

You get a personal report

Right after the study you see your own decision profile: acceptance rate, sensitivity to AI confidence, accuracy vs. a baseline. Then compare to the aggregate on the dashboard.

What this study is really about

TrustLab is a small vehicle for a big question in cognitive science and human–AI interaction: how well do people calibrate their trust in AI advice?

Algorithm appreciation vs. aversion

Do people over-rely on the AI even when it is confidently wrong? Or under-use it even when it is confidently right?

Confidence as a cue

Does a higher stated confidence from the AI shift acceptance, holding correctness constant?

Response time as a signal

Faster decisions may indicate reliance on the AI; slower ones may indicate deliberation.

Calibration gap

Are participants better off deferring to the AI, overriding it, or applying a discerning mix?
